Theoretical condensed matter physics

Amogh Anakru

Ph.D. student in Physics at Penn State University

I study strongly correlated quantum matter, quantum field theory in many-body systems, generalized symmetries, and tensor-network methods. Much of my recent work concerns dipolar and other modulated symmetries.

I am advised by Prof. Zhen Bi and expect to complete my Ph.D. in May 2027. Before Penn State, I earned a B.S. in Engineering Physics from Cornell University.

Research

My work lies at the intersection of condensed matter theory, quantum field theory, and quantum information. Current directions include:

Dipolar and modulated symmetries

Symmetry breaking, topology, and low-energy field theories in systems with spatially modulated conservation laws.

Gapless topological matter

One-dimensional gapless phases, topological distinctions, and Lifshitz criticality in dipole-conserving fermionic chains.

Strongly correlated metals

Non-Fermi-liquid behavior and emergent gauge-field signatures in dipolar and composite-fermion metals.

Tensor networks

Matrix-product-state descriptions of SPT phases and Lieb–Schultz–Mattis constraints for modulated symmetries.
